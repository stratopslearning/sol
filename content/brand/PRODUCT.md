# SOL product

Canonical product description for marketing, SEO/AIO, docs, and LinkedIn. Prefer this over older one-liners that center Canvas or grading alone.

## In one sentence

SOL is the operating system for teaching: an AI layer that takes over the repetitive work around running a course, so instructors spend their time on teaching.

## Positioning

| Line | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **The operating system for teaching.** | Brand / hero tagline |
| **The AI teaching layer for the LMS you already use.** | SEO title, meta, cold audiences |
| **Teaching, in a better light.** | Warm secondary / brand phrase |

SOL is its own layer. It is not “an LMS,” not “a Canvas product,” and not “an AI quiz grader.”

## The problem

Teaching has a lot of work around it. Instructors build materials, write assignments, grade, answer the same student questions, work out who is struggling, then redo much of it next term. Most of that work is manual, and AI tools tend to add to it, because students can now copy answers.

## What SOL does

Five modules form a loop:

1. **Knowledge** — A workspace-shared library of the instructor’s materials (uploads, URLs, and synced Drive / OneDrive / SharePoint / Notion / Confluence), versioned with OCR when needed. Courses attach documents from that library; everything else draws on those attachments.
2. **Authoring** — Builds the course and its assignments from that material. In “previous course” mode, you give it last term’s course plus new materials. It keeps the same outline, improves it, and writes similar assignments with different questions so students don’t get recycled work.
3. **Learning and tutor** — Students get help from a tutor that answers only from the course materials, plus interactive formats that make copying harder.
4. **Assessment** — Short-answer, scenario, and explain-your-reasoning questions graded against the instructor’s rubric, with the reasoning shown and uncertain answers sent to the instructor for review.
5. **Analytics** — Shows which concepts the class struggled with, so the next term improves. This feeds back into Authoring.

Grading is one part of Assessment. The product is the whole loop: **build, teach, assess, and improve.**

Instructors approve; SOL drafts and suggests.

## How it fits into existing tools

SOL is its own layer, and it connects to the tools people already use.

- **Canvas** is the first connector (optional): launch, roster sync, and grade passback when LTI is enabled.
- **Other LMSs** can follow.
- **Without an LMS:** SOL can run for corporate or small-business training.
- **MCP:** instructors can ask an AI assistant to create a quiz, copy it to sections, or check the gradebook.

**Commercial segments (one product):** Creator (individual courses) → Business (onboarding/training) → University department → Institution / multi-campus. Engineering tenancy and billing: [`docs/architecture/scale-and-tenancy.md`](../../docs/architecture/scale-and-tenancy.md).

Do not lead marketing with Canvas. Mention connectors as optional fit, not the product identity.

## SEO / AIO copy rules

- Lead with what it is (OS for teaching / AI teaching layer) and who it helps (instructors running courses).
- Name the loop (Knowledge → Authoring → Learning → Assessment → Analytics).
- Say LMS-agnostic; Canvas only as optional integration.
- Never reduce the product to grading alone.
