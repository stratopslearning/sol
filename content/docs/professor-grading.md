# Grading and Attention

SOL grades with **rubric matches from the model** and **scores from application code**. You remain the grader of record. Unsure answers wait in **Attention** instead of quietly inventing a score.

## How short-answer grading works

1. The model returns which rubric criteria the student met (and rationale)
2. Application code computes the numeric score from those matches
3. If the model is unsure, or the AI path is unavailable, the attempt surfaces for **manual review**
4. You can accept, adjust, or regrade from the attempt page or Attention queue

This keeps grading auditable: every score should be explainable from criterion-level evidence.

## Attention queue

**Attention** collects attempts that need a human eye: pending review, manual grading, or fallback paths.

Typical actions:

- Open the attempt and read the student answer + rubric matches
- Adjust the score when you disagree
- Regrade when materials or rubrics changed
- Resolve items so they leave the queue

## Discussions

AI-evaluated discussions follow a similar pattern: the model evaluates against your prompts/rubric; you can audit sessions and intervene when needed.

## What students see

Students see scores and feedback you have released through the normal results / grades flow. They do not see internal Attention state.

## Honesty about AI

- Say “SOL suggests” and “you review,” not “SOL automatically knows”
- Prefer criterion language over opaque total scores alone
- Use [Analytics](/docs/professor-analytics) after graded work to see which concepts to reteach
